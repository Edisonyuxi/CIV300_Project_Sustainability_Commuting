Main Page References (Data & Thresholds)

  1. Hannah Ritchie (2023). “Which form of transport has the smallest carbon footprint?” Our World in Data. Based on the UK “Greenhouse gas reporting: conversion factors 2022”, providing per-passenger-km factors for petrol cars, national rail and domestic flights. Link
  2. Toronto Environmental Alliance (2010). “Clearing the Air on the TTC”. Table 1 reports greenhouse gas emissions per 1 million passenger-km for diesel buses (116.6 t), light rail vehicles (39 t) and subways (29 t), converted here to 117, 39 and 29 g CO₂e per passenger-km. Link
  3. International Union of Railways (UIC). “Carbon markets and rail” white paper. Reports rail well-to-wheel emissions around 22 g CO₂ per passenger-km, supporting the use of a low emission factor for trains. Link
  4. Hot or Cool Institute (2021). “1.5-Degree Lifestyles: Towards a Fair Consumption Space for All”. Proposes lifestyle carbon footprint targets of 2.5 tCO₂e/person·year by 2030 and 0.7 tCO₂e by 2050. The 2.5 tCO₂e/year target (~6.8 kg/day) is used to derive the daily commuting thresholds for the Earth mood, assuming ~30% of the budget is allocated to personal transport. Link
  5. EcoSchools Canada (2024). “The Car in Carbon Dioxide”. Summarizes that a typical private vehicle emits about 4.6 tonnes of CO₂ per year, consistent with U.S. EPA data, providing Canadian context for private car emissions. Link

Note: exact emissions depend on vehicle technology, occupancy and electricity mix. This page uses these sources to choose representative per-passenger-kilometre factors and a 1.5 °C-aligned daily budget so users can compare different commuting choices.
