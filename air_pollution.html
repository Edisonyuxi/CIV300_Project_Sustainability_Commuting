1. Information

CO₂ & local pollutants · Toronto private vehicles

Transport emissions change the chemical composition of the lower atmosphere. Internal combustion engine vehicles (ICEVs) emit carbon dioxide (CO₂) from fuel combustion, as well as local air pollutants such as nitrogen oxides (NOₓ), fine particulate matter (PM₂.₅), carbon monoxide (CO), and volatile organic compounds (VOCs). These pollutants contribute to smog formation and adverse health impacts.

In this page, we focus on a simple but important part of the problem: annual CO₂ emissions from different classes of private vehicles in the Toronto region. Using typical fuel consumption (L/100 km), yearly distance travelled (km/year) and fuel emission factors (kg CO₂/L), we can estimate how much CO₂ an average vehicle emits in a year, and then scale this up using the number of vehicles in each class.

Per-vehicle annual CO₂ model

  • Fuel emission factors (including common ethanol blends): gasoline ≈ 2.3 kg CO₂/L, diesel ≈ 2.7 kg CO₂/L.
  • Annual emissions per vehicle: CO₂ (t/vehicle·year) = d × FC × EF / 1000, where d = annual distance (km/year), FC = fuel consumption (L/100 km), EF = emission factor (kg CO₂/L).
  • City-wide emissions for each class: CO₂total = CO₂per vehicle × number of vehicles.

The numbers below are stylized estimates for Toronto in 2024 based on federal data. They focus on CO₂ from tailpipe fuel use only; upstream emissions (fuel production) and non-CO₂ pollutants are not included here.
