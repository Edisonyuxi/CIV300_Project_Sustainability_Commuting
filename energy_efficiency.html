1. Information

Combustion engines convert the chemical energy stored in gasoline or diesel into mechanical work to move vehicles. However, a large fraction of this energy is lost as waste heat through the exhaust, engine block, and friction in the drivetrain.

Electric drivetrains, especially when powered by low-carbon electricity, can achieve much higher end-use efficiency because less energy is lost as heat in the motor and gearbox.

Key relationships (placeholders):

  • Energy efficiency: η = Useful Energy Out / Energy In
  • Typical values used in the main page sliders:
    • Internal combustion engine (ICE): ηICE ≈ 20–35%
    • Electric drivetrain: ηEV ≈ 80–95%
  • Relative improvement: Δη ≈ ηEV − ηICE

* For a complete analysis, you may extend this to a “well-to-wheel” or lifecycle perspective, including electricity generation and fuel production.

3. References

  1. Electricity Carbon Intensity in Ontario (kWh → CO₂)
    Ontario’s grid is among the cleanest in North America due to nuclear and hydro.

    • IESO (Independent Electricity System Operator): Ontario 2023 grid intensity ≈ 33 g CO₂/kWh
    • Government of Canada (GHGenius Dataset): ≈ 30–40 g CO₂/kWh
    • Ontario Energy Statistics: NRCan Comprehensive Energy Tables

    → For transit energy → CO₂ calculations, 33 g/kWh is used.

  2. TTC Streetcar Energy Use (Toronto)

    • Typical streetcar energy intensity (North America): ~0.15–0.18 kWh per passenger-km
    • NREL Data: Energy Consumption Dataset
    • Ryerson University LCA Study (“Comparative Life Cycle Assessment of Public Transit Toronto”): Full Study

    Conservative estimate used: 0.17 kWh/p-km → 0.17 × 33 = 5.6 g CO₂ per passenger-km.

  3. TTC Subway Energy Use

    • Electric heavy rail with regenerative braking
    • Typical energy intensity: 0.10–0.14 kWh per passenger-km
    • Government of Canada – Rail Energy Study: Energy Efficiency Resources

    Toronto average: 0.12 kWh/p-km → 0.12 × 33 = 3.96 g CO₂ per passenger-km.

  4. TTC Electric & Hybrid Bus Energy Use

    • BYD / Nova electric bus pilot – City of Toronto report: Electric Bus Performance Results
    • Energy consumption measured: 0.9–1.2 kWh per vehicle-km
    • Typical occupancy: ~20 passengers/km (TTC Occupancy Data)

    Per passenger-km: 1.0 ÷ 20 = 0.05 kWh/p-km → 0.05 × 33 = 1.65 g CO₂ per passenger-km.

  5. Biking & Walking

    • Human metabolic energy: ≈ 0.03 kWh per km
    • Local operational emissions: 0 g CO₂
