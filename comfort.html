1. Information

Human thermal comfort depends on a combination of air temperature, humidity, wind speed, radiation, clothing and activity level. In urban streets and transit corridors, heat exposure can be amplified by the urban heat island effect.

Common metrics used in this course include the heat index or the Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI), which combine meteorological variables into a single “feels-like” temperature.

Example simplified relationship (placeholder):

  • Apparent temperature used in the main page: Tapp ≈ T + f(RH) − g(Wind) + ΔTUHI where:
    • T = air temperature (°C)
    • RH = relative humidity (%)
    • Wind = wind speed (m/s)
    • ΔTUHI = urban heat island temperature increase

2. Creative Component

Infographic: Same Toronto Day, Different Commute

This infographic compares comfort across four commute types—car, bus stop, subway, and bike—under the exact same Toronto weather conditions.

  • Car: Temperature stable due to AC, but air quality drops quickly in congestion.[H2]
  • Bus Stop: Full exposure to sun, humidity, wind; comfort varies widely.[H1]
  • Subway: Stable, protected, air-conditioned environment.[H2]
  • Bike: Best air quality and freedom of movement, but requires physical effort.[H2]

Different commuting modes affect how hot or cold we feel even under the same outdoor temperature, because comfort depends on more than just the air temperature. Exposure to sunlight, humidity, wind, surrounding surfaces, traffic heat, and physical effort shapes the “feels-like” experience during travel.
